# 02 - Vision Brief

## Purpose

Lock the game's identity before design expands. This is the first formal approval gate. Everything downstream references this document.

## Vision Paragraph

<!-- One paragraph. What is this game? Who is it for? What does the player feel? What makes it distinct? -->
<!-- Must be expressible without jargon, lists, or hedge words. -->

A calm, garden-themed math practice web app for Berlin second-graders that turns curriculum-aligned practice into a caring routine. Children complete short, varied sessions that grow living plants, wake sleeping ones, and build confidence without pressure. The product stands out by combining real Grade 2 mastery tracking with harmless motivation and clear progress for both children and parents.

## Creative Pillars

<!-- 3–5 non-negotiable design principles. Each pillar should pass the test: "Does this decision strengthen or weaken pillar X?" -->
<!-- Format: Pillar name — one sentence definition. -->

1. Honest Grade 2 Progress - Progress should show real Berlin Grade 2 mastery, not just finished exercises.
2. Motivation Without Pressure - The garden should encourage return practice through care, delight, and recoverable sleeping states, never through speed rewards, fear of loss, or shame.
3. Learning That Sticks - Sessions should build lasting understanding through short, varied, review-aware practice, not short-lived novelty.
4. Simple for Children, Clear for Parents - The child experience should stay calm and understandable, while parents can still see whether Grade 2 expectations are becoming solid.
5. Great at Grade 2 First - The first release should do Berlin Grade 2 practice exceptionally well before expanding to other grades or heavier product layers.

## Target Player

- **Who they are**: Berlin elementary school children practicing Grade 2 math, often with support or oversight from parents who want confidence that practice is worthwhile.
- **What they want to feel**: Capable, calm, motivated, and proud of steady progress.
- **What experiences may appeal to them**: Simple educational apps, light care or collection mechanics, and playful progression that feels understandable rather than overwhelming.
- **Why they would choose this**: It promises trustworthy Grade 2-aligned practice with a caring, non-punitive motivation loop instead of repetitive drill or pressure-heavy gamification.

## Success Criteria

<!-- What does a successful version of this game look like? How would we know it worked? -->
<!-- Measurable or observable where possible. -->

1. Children return for short practice sessions over multiple weeks, not just during the first burst of novelty.
2. Parents can clearly see which Grade 2 expectations are solid, in progress, or ready for review.
3. Practice feels supportive and low-pressure while leading to measurable improvement in covered Grade 2 skills.
4. The first release ships with a narrow Grade 2 scope, high exercise quality, clear metadata, and coherent UX.

## Non-Goals

<!-- What this game deliberately is not. What it will not try to be. -->
<!-- These are as important as the pillars. -->

1. The first release is not a full grades 1-6 product.
2. It is not a high-stimulation story game or entertainment-first garden simulator.
3. It does not use speed rewards, perfection badges, public competition, or coercive streaks as core motivation.
4. It is not a heavy teacher-admin or analytics platform in version one, even if it includes limited progress visibility for adults.

## Core Trade-Off Priorities

<!-- When pillars conflict, which wins? Explicit priority ordering. -->
<!-- Example: "Challenge over accessibility. Atmosphere over performance. Replayability over content volume." -->

1. Real learning over engagement tricks.
2. Calm clarity over spectacle.
3. Grade 2 depth over broad coverage.
4. Flexible mastery evidence over rigid grade-only progression.
